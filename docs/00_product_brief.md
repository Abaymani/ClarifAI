# Product brief
ClarifAI is a tool that aims to aid students in upper secondary school reflect on their AI usage during practical, project-based learning activities. The tool is meant to help students become more cognitively aware of how their use of AI can influence their learning, rather than restricting their usage. The tools is meant to help them in the long run, as they most likely will interact with AI in the future. 

## Task
The task that the students will perform is an open-ended practical project, where the students will get to build whatever they fancy with Elegoos (Uno R3 platform) and an array of components provided by us. The students will have unlimited access to Google Gemini to help them in their projects. The students will have limited prior knowledge on how to build with microcontrollers, which is where Gemini can help the students. The programming language is most likely completely new to the students, as they haven't been taught C++ prior. Therefore the students are allowed to prompt the AI to generate their code for them, as it is not a part of the intended learning outcomes for the project. The tool should not determine whether the use of AI is appropriate, but rather help the students reflect on how they use it. 

## Tool Use-case
The students will upload their transcripts and then the tool will analyze their transcript and flag prompts and behaviours that might be signs of cognitive offloading. The transcript will consist of the students prompts and the AI-responses. The tool will analyze the transcripts from the student according to a framework of behaviours and sequences that we have provided. The framework is based on the ICAP-framework. The analysis should be focused around the user, analyzing their prompts and observable patterns in the students interaction with the AI. 

After the analysis of the transcripts, the tool with present the students with some examples from their own transcripts. Theses examples should be concrete examples from their own transcripts that should be accompanied with questions to help the student reflect on how the AI-usage influenced their reasoning, decision-making and understanding during the project. ClarifAI does not grade the student nor make clear conclusions that cognitive offloading, as it should only flag the potential cognitive offloading and let the student reflect on those prompts. The tool should not determine whether a student's behaviour is good or bad, as some cognitive offloading can be productive in some situations. The purpose is to highlight the actions that might be signs of cognitive offloading and let the student reflect on whether it was an active choice or if it was subconsciously that the student might have cognitively offloaded. The tool can also emphasize good prompting behaviours, such behaviours that show great cognitive engagement. If there is uncertainty in the analysis, the system should not make overconfident claims to the student, who has more context about their work than the transcript can provide.. 

The tool is meant as a reflection aid, not as an assessment system. The important aspect is not to focus on to what extent the students use the AI, but how the students interact with AI. The tool should not make claims on the students overall cognitive state, only highlighting actual observed patterns from the submitted transcripts. The tool should not consider whether the students code, circuit or other technical solution is correct, as the technical correctness is not the primary focus of the system. 

For research purposes, the tool should also scrape the students transcripts so that they can be analysed further without the risk of the transcript being deleted. The transcript handed in will be a link to their chat with Google Gemini without external pre-prompting, unless the student pre-prompts their chat. The student will use the tool at the end of each lesson, and will upload a link to their transcript to start the session.
