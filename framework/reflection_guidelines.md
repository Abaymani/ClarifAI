# Choosing Reflection Examples and Questions

These guidelines are used after the transcript has been coded. They say how to choose the observations the student reflects on, and how each behaviour group's texts (`behaviour_grouping.JSON`) are used.

## Choosing observations

- Choose at most the number of observations you are given, and only from the listed observations.
- Prefer observations that are clearly supported, concrete and worth this student's time to think about. Patterns the framework marks as important weigh more:
  - S1 is high priority, S2 and S3 are important moment for reflection, and S7 should always be reinforced.
- Mix reflection (R groups) and reinforcement (F groups) when the transcript allows it, and avoid choosing two observations from the same group.
  - F5 and F9 are considered the most important groups to reinforce.
  - R1, R2 and R4 are considered the most important groups to reflect on.
- If none of the observations is suitable, choose none. The student then gets the general questions (R0) instead, since the transcript didn't give enough evidence for specific observations.

## Writing the questions

- The intro and the questions are the ones of the observation's group, used as the researchers wrote them. Only the parts in square brackets are filled in.
- Write everything in Swedish and address the student as "du". Never show code names or ICAP labels to the student.
- Don't grade the student, don't judge their AI use as good or bad overall, and don't assess whether their code, circuit or solution is correct.
